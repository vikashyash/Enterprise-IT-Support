# MFA and suspicious sign-in guidance

Use this document if you see an unexpected MFA prompt, a sign-in you do not recognise, or you think someone else used your account.

## What to do immediately

1. Do not approve a surprise MFA prompt. Press **Deny** and, if the app offers it, **This wasn't me**.
2. Change your password through the official self-service reset page, not through a link in email.
3. Tell the support agent you have a security concern, or email security@aethertech.example from a different device if you cannot sign in.
4. Stay on that other device until Security says the account is clean.

## What counts as suspicious

- MFA prompts while you are not signing in
- A sign-in mail from a city or country you have not visited
- Outlook rules you did not create, especially forwarding to an external address
- A new device listed under your Microsoft 365 account that you do not own

## What you must not do

- Do not send screenshots of authenticator backup codes in chat.
- Do not turn MFA off. Security will never ask you to disable MFA.
- Do not keep working in the possibly compromised session.

## What happens next

Security reviews the sign-in log, revokes sessions if needed, and tells you when you may sign in again. Employee-facing guidance stops here. Internal containment steps are in the restricted security playbook and are not published to all staff.
